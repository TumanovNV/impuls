# Website Legal and Privacy Localization

## Scope

This document owns the engineering contract for public privacy/legal pages. It is not legal advice and it is not a certification of compliance in any jurisdiction. The public pages must describe the **actual Impuls data flows** and must not claim a legal status that has not been established.

The product privacy facts come from [`PRIVACY.md`](../../PRIVACY.md) and the current source/application behavior. Legal pages may summarize those facts but may not invent a new network owner, telemetry field, retention period, recipient or identifier.

## Public legal routes

`Scripts/site-locales/registry.json` owns both the marketing `path` and the legal `privacy_path` for every published locale.

```text
ru       /                 /privacy/
en       /en/              /en/privacy/
de       /de/              /de/privacy/
fr       /fr/              /fr/privacy/
es       /es/              /es/privacy/
ja       /ja/              /ja/privacy/
zh-Hans  /zh-hans/         /zh-hans/privacy/
```

Never derive `privacy_path` from the BCP-47 code. `zh-Hans` is again the counterexample.

`/site-privacy.html` is a legacy URL only. `Scripts/sync-site-privacy-legacy.py` turns it into a `noindex,follow` handoff to `/privacy/`; it must not remain an indexed sitemap URL.

## Source, revision metadata and translations

The Russian `/privacy/` page is the operator's source policy. The six other legal pages are translations / localized notices for clarity.

All seven pages are generated by one `Scripts/build-site-privacy-locale.py` from:

- `Scripts/site-privacy-template.html` — presentation template;
- `Scripts/site-privacy-locales/<locale>.json` — legal copy;
- `Scripts/site-locales/registry.json` — route and language cluster.

Policy-level metadata is separate from translated prose. `Scripts/site-privacy-locales/metadata.json` owns the machine-readable source locale, policy `revision` and ISO `effective_date`. On the current baseline it records revision `3.0`, effective `2026-08-23`.

That effective date also owns legal sitemap freshness. `Scripts/sync-site-sitemap.py` must project it as `<lastmod>` for **all seven privacy URLs**. It must not reuse the old legacy-page date and must not use wall-clock “today” as a substitute for the actual policy revision date.

Generated privacy HTML under `docs/**/privacy/index.html` is not edited directly.

Translation status on this baseline:

- RU — source policy;
- EN — international translation;
- DE / FR / ES / JA / zh-Hans — AI-assisted translations with technical/structural review;
- no native-speaker legal/linguistic review is claimed for the translated legal copy.

Public pages explicitly say that mandatory rights under applicable local law are not reduced by a translation and that the notice is not a universal compliance certification.

## Russian-law baseline

The source policy is built around the actual processing and the Russian personal-data framework, including Federal Law No. 152-FZ. Current public legal references use the official `pravo.gov.ru` source where possible and Roskomnadzor's personal-data portal for cross-border-transfer information.

The policy must continue to distinguish:

- local app data that never reaches the operator;
- GitHub-hosted website/download/feedback processing;
- optional first-party version statistics, disabled until explicit user consent;
- user-initiated web-player processing by the selected provider.

Do not describe the version-statistics pseudonym as anonymous. `PRIVACY.md` treats it as personal data where applicable law requires that classification.

## GitHub Pages fact

GitHub's current Pages documentation states that a visitor IP address is logged and stored for security purposes when a GitHub Pages site is visited, regardless of sign-in state. The Impuls policy therefore says exactly that and separately says that the Impuls site code has no first-party form, advertising pixel or connected web-analytics service.

Do not replace this with the stronger/false statement that “the website collects no personal data at all”. Hosting-level network processing still exists.

## EEA / GDPR supplement

The translated notice includes a narrow GDPR supplement because an operator outside the EU can fall within GDPR territorial scope when processing relates to offering goods or services to people in the EU.

The public wording intentionally avoids “GDPR compliant” or equivalent certification language.

Current operator-controlled bases described by the notice when GDPR applies:

- optional version statistics — consent (`Article 6(1)(a)`); disabling the setting stops future requests;
- voluntarily initiated support requests — processing to answer the user's request and the project's legitimate interests in product support/security, subject to applicable rights;
- GitHub hosting/network processing — described as GitHub's own infrastructure processing under GitHub's privacy statement, not silently re-labelled as an Impuls analytics flow.

The notice lists the common GDPR data-subject rights conditionally (“where GDPR applies / in the circumstances provided by GDPR”) and states that Impuls has no advertising profiles or solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.

## Known international legal gaps

There is currently **no dedicated private privacy email address published for Impuls**. The policy therefore does not invent one.

The public initial-contact route is GitHub, with a strong warning that Issues are public and must not contain identity documents, address, phone, email, installation ID or other confidential data. If verification/additional data is required, the operator must arrange a private channel with the requester.

This is a known legal/UX gap, particularly for international privacy requests. A future dedicated private privacy mailbox can improve the notice, but documentation must not claim that mailbox exists until it is actually operational.

No DPO or EU representative is claimed. If GDPR Article 3(2) applies to Impuls, Article 27 representative requirements require a dedicated legal assessment before any complete-GDPR-compliance claim. The project must not silently assume the narrow Article 27(2) exception; recurring opt-in version-statistics processing is one reason the “occasional processing” question needs explicit review rather than inference.

Article 13 contact/representative disclosure follows the same truth rule: do not invent private mailbox, DPO or representative details before they actually exist.

## Data facts that legal copy must preserve

Current first-party version-statistics contract:

- explicit opt-in;
- fixed first-party endpoint;
- at most one heartbeat attempt per hour;
- random installation UUID, current version and available real previous version only;
- raw UUID replaced by server-side HMAC before product database storage;
- IP/User-Agent are not product analytics fields;
- record retention: 365 days without a heartbeat;
- no clipboard, notes, snippets, file paths, calendar, music, device names, serials, UDIDs or other user content.

Feedback contract:

- report prepared locally;
- complete report shown before action;
- Impuls does not upload it;
- user opens a public GitHub form and submits there under GitHub's terms.

## SEO / hreflang contract

Every legal page is a real static indexable document with:

- localized `<html lang>`;
- localized title and description;
- self-canonical URL;
- reciprocal legal-page `hreflang` for all seven locales;
- `x-default` pointing to `/privacy/`;
- the same nine-section structural contract.

`docs/sitemap.xml` lists all seven landing pages and all seven privacy pages. Every privacy entry uses `metadata.json.effective_date` as its `lastmod`. The old `/site-privacy.html` URL is intentionally absent from `<loc>` entries.

## Pipeline

```text
registry.json ──────────────────────────────────────────┐
                                                       │
site-privacy-locales/<locale>.json ─┐                  │
site-privacy-template.html ──────────┼─> build-site-privacy-locale.py
                                    │                  │
                                    └────> docs/<privacy_path>/index.html
                                                       │
site-privacy-locales/metadata.json ────────────────┐   │
                                                   └─> sync-site-sitemap.py
sync-site-privacy-links.py ─────────────> landing CTA → localized privacy route
sync-site-sitemap.py ───────────────────> landing + legal sitemap cluster
sync-site-privacy-legacy.py ─────────────> /site-privacy.html legacy handoff
```

`.github/workflows/site-legal-localization.yml` is the focused PR gate. `.github/workflows/site-release-sync.yml` generates and commits the actual static legal pages after merge and after relevant website-generator changes.

## Required checks

Before merge:

1. all seven privacy locale JSON files parse;
2. each has the same nine sections and TOC IDs;
3. route/config parity matches `registry.json`;
4. every generated privacy page has self-canonical + seven reciprocal hreflangs + x-default;
5. `/zh-hans/privacy/` is built from `zh-Hans`, never from a guessed path;
6. the landing privacy CTA points to `privacy/` so generated landing pages resolve to their own local privacy child;
7. sitemap contains all seven privacy routes and excludes legacy `site-privacy.html` from `<loc>`;
8. every privacy sitemap entry uses the policy `effective_date` from `metadata.json`;
9. legacy page is `noindex,follow` and points to `/privacy/`;
10. GitHub/HMAC/365-day/PRIVACY.md/GDPR factual anchors remain present;
11. knowledge-base and the full application build remain green.
